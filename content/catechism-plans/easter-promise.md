## **Session Title: From Shadow to Splendor**
**Target:** 7th/8th Grade

### **I. Opening & Hook**
* **Ice Breaker:** "Do you have a character in a book, movie, or cartoon that you feared in childhood? Why?" 
    * *Goal:* Acknowledge that "darkness" usually wears a face when we are young.
* **Media Clip:** Harry Potter vs. Voldemort. 
    * *The Pivot:* Ask them: "In the clip, what does the light represent? Is it just a weapon, or is it something more?"
* **Analogy: The Formless & The Void:**
    * *Bridge:* "In Harry Potter, light overcomes darkness. But in the Bible, light does something even more fundamental—it *creates* order out of chaos. Before God said 'Let there be light,' the world wasn't evil—it was just... nothing. Let's explore what that 'nothing' was like."
    * To explain the "void" before light, use the **"Missing Code"** or **"Dark Room"** analogy:
        * **The Unfinished Game:** Imagine a video game with no light engine rendered. You can’t see the walls, the floor, or the objective. Everything exists potentially, but it has no *order*. Light is the "command" that makes the world readable.
        * **The Sculptor:** Darkness is the block of marble; Light is the chisel that reveals the figure inside.
        * **Biological Analogies:** A seed in the dirt is in "darkness"—it is "formless" potential. The sun (Light) is the signal that tells the seed *how* to become a flower. Without light, the seed stays a "nothing."
    * *The Deeper Question:* "So is darkness powerful? Here's the truth: darkness has NO power. It's literally nothing. The 'power' of darkness only comes from OUR fear. Think about it—why do we fear the dark? Because we're made for light! We were created by God who shines into our lives. Our love for God's light is what makes us hate darkness. That's why we personify darkness as 'the devil'—it's our way of giving a face to the nothingness we were never meant to be in."
    * **CCC 298:** "Since God could create everything out of nothing, he can also, through the Holy Spirit, give spiritual life to sinners by creating a pure heart in them, and bodily life to the dead through the Resurrection. God 'gives life to the dead and calls into existence the things that do not exist.' And since God was able to make light shine in darkness by his Word, he can also give the light of faith to those who do not yet know him."

### **II. The Scriptural Journey: Five Stages of Light**

**Creation**
* Ask candidates to turn to Genesis 1:1-4, and invite someone to proclaim the passage OR a few people to proclaim one verse each.
> In the beginning when God created the heavens and the earth, the earth was a formless void and darkness covered the face of the deep, while the spirit of God swept over the face of the waters. Then God said, "Let there be light"; and there was light. And God saw that the light was good; and God separated the light from the darkness.
* **Activity:** Eyes closed, hands over eyes. Read the passage slowly, then invite them to open their eyes.
* **Discussion:** Why do you think God created light first? (There can be no life without light, look at plants for example)
* God created life out of nothing through the Holy Spirit.
* In the same way, God gives spiritual life to sinners through the Holy Spirit (Cf. CCC 298). We see this when the Easter candle is used to bless the water for baptism, just as the Holy Spirit swept over the waters at the beginning.

**Exodus**
* Ask candidates to turn to Exodus 13:21, and invite someone to proclaim the passage.
> The Lord went in front of them in a pillar of cloud by day, to lead them along the way, and in a pillar of fire by night, to give them light, so that they might travel by day and by night.
* Where do you recognise this pillar of fire in the Easter Vigil liturgy? (The Paschal/Easter candle)
* The candle is an image of Christ our Light. In the Book of Exodus, we are taught how Christ was mystically present in the Exodus as the true light who led Israel out of Egypt through the darkness of night.
* These two images of the pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire come together in the Easter Vigil. Although we begin outside, we are led into the Church first by the pillar of cloud (symbolized by the incense at the head of the procession) and followed by the pillar of fire (the Paschal candle).
* Just as the ancient Israelites went forth in the Exodus out of slavery into freedom, we are led out of the darkness of sin into the light of new life through baptism.
* The candle goes ahead of the congregation into the darkened church; how can we apply this imagery to our own lives? (We need the light of Christ to lead us in our journey).
* **CCC 1243:** "The candle, lit from the Easter candle, signifies that Christ has enlightened the neophyte. In him the baptized are 'the light of the world.' The newly baptized is now, in the only Son, a child of God entitled to say the prayer of the children of God: 'Our Father.'"

**The Incarnation**
* Ask candidates to turn to Isaiah 9:2, 4, 6; and invite someone to proclaim the verses OR a few people to proclaim one verse each.
> The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light; those who lived in a land of deep darkness—on them light has shined.
> For the yoke of their burden, and the bar across their shoulders, the rod of their oppressor, you have broken as on the day of Midian.
> For a child has been born for us, a son given to us; authority rests upon his shoulders; and he is named Wonderful Counsellor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace.
* Who do you think this prophecy was pointing to? (Jesus Christ)
* Christ is the light that shines in the darkness to lead his people out of the oppression of sin.
* The light also reveals what is hidden, so Christ came to reveal God to humanity as "the image of the invisible God" (Colossians 1:15).
* Christ is the light which allows humanity to see the face of God and come to know him as a loving Father.

**The Resurrection**
* Ask candidates to turn to John 1:1-5, and invite someone to proclaim the passage OR a few people to proclaim one verse each.
> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things came into being through him, and without him not one thing came into being. What has come into being in him was life, and the life was the light of all people. The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it.
* Who do you think is the Word who is also the light? (Jesus Christ)
* "The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it." What do you think this verse is referring to? (The death and resurrection of Jesus, whose light is stronger than death).
* Proclaim Colossians 1:18
> He is the head of the body, the church; he is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, so that he might come to have first place in everything.
* The Good News is since Christ is the head and we are the body, we also will share in his life. We need not fear death because (invite the youths to say it together): the light of Christ is stronger than death.

**Parousia**
* Ask candidates to turn to Revelation 19:6-8, and invite someone to proclaim the passage OR a few people to proclaim one verse each.
> Then I heard what seemed to be the voice of a great multitude, like the sound of many waters and like the sound of mighty thunder peals, crying out, "Hallelujah! For our Lord God, the Almighty reigns. Let us rejoice and celebrate and give Him the glory. For the marriage of the Lamb has come, and His bride has made herself ready. She was given clothing of fine linen, linen bright and pure." For the fine linen she wears is the righteousness of the saints.
* At Parousia, a marriage will take place between Christ and the Church, who will be adorned like a bride on her wedding day.
* This is foreshadowed in the Easter Vigil liturgy, as the flame of the paschal candle is shared, a darkened Church is clothed in light. The Exsultet, or Easter Proclamation, is read or sung by the deacon or priest at the arrival of the candle in the sanctuary. One part is as we have heard sung earlier: Rejoice, let Mother Church also rejoice, arrayed with the lightning of his glory.
* Just as in Heaven we will not be arrayed in garments of mere cloth but of light like we heard in Revelation, the Church now see herself clothed in light, the Lord's light.
* So in the Easter celebration we also anticipate the Parousia, where God will be all in all.

### **III. The Story of St. John Henry Newman**

**The Transition:** "We’ve talked about the big history of the world. But what about when *your* life feels dark? When you don’t know what to do next? There was a brilliant man named John Henry Newman who found himself literally stuck in the dark."

* **The Story:** In 1833, Newman was traveling in Sicily. He became dangerously ill (fever) and was stuck on a boat in the middle of a fog. He was physically weak and spiritually confused about his path in the church. 
* **The Struggle:** He didn't have the "whole map." He just wanted to see the "next step."
* **The Lesson:** He realized he didn't need to see the distant scene (the future). He just needed enough light for the *next* step.

---

### **IV. Closing & Prayer**

* **Final Reflection:** Ask them: "What is one 'darkness' in your life or the world right now that needs a 'next step' of light?"
* **Closing Song/Prayer:** Listen to or read the lyrics of **"Lead, Kindly Light."**

> *Lead, Kindly Light, amid the encircling gloom,*
> *Lead Thou me on!*
> *The night is dark, and I am far from home—*
> *Lead Thou me on!*
> *Keep Thou my feet; I do not ask to see*
> *The distant scene—one step enough for me.*

* **Action:** Give them a small tea-light candle (unlit) to take home as a reminder that they carry a piece of that "Easter Promise."

---

### **Pro-Tips for 7/8th Graders:**
* **Keep it moving:** If a discussion stalls, move to the next scripture.
* **Validate the "Voldemort" fears:** Don't dismiss their childhood fears; use them to show that as we grow, our fears get "bigger" (loneliness, failure, death), and that’s why we need a bigger Light.
* **Participation:** Let them handle the Bible. Finding the verses themselves keeps them engaged.
